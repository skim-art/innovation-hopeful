Books are simply the best. They're pathways to other worlds, pictures of quiet afternoons. They can be colorful or black type on a page, but regardless, books are valuable and deserve to be treated that way.

If you live in Houston and you've recently done a deep clean of your bookshelf and found yourself with a pile of books (in good condition!) that you've aged out of or don't want anymore, you have several options:

    Books for Development: This organization takes your donated books and distributes them in developing countries to promote literacy. Books can be any language, but books in English, African languages, and Spanish are the most valuable. There are several ways to donate:
      Dropoff at 3709 Polk St, Houston, TX 77003 (their sorting warehouse) on Saturday from 9am-12pm (it's very fast and easy!). Call ahead to let them know that you're coming. Create a Book Box - essentially, a mobile library that will be in Africa. It involves a cash payment to set it up and centers around a specific theme (books curated by you). Learn more here. "Large Donation Pick Up" - mostly from schools and libraries.
    Find out more about their mission and what they will or won't accept on their website.     Books Between Kids: This nonprofit helps kids living in families that live on or below the poverty line in Harris County (Houston, Texas) gain access to books. Their goal is to keep kids on track at school and build the kids' home libraries. There are a couple ways to donate:
      Dropoff small donations (their website says the size of a grocery bag) at one of these locations. Alternatively, you can drop off large donations at the Books Between Kids warehouse (9947 Harwin Dr. Suite B & C Houston, TX 77036) by scheduling a time here. You can also organize a book drive, for which Books Between Kids has resources here.
    Find out more about what they accept on their website.     Better World Books: This business resells your donated books on their online website and states that "proceeds fund literary partners worldwide," "enpower[ing] communities and promot[ing] a circular economy for books." They collect books via Dropboxes, which are in the locations listed on their website here. Kaboom Books Bookstore: If you would like to make money off of your donations, you can sell (or trade) used books to Kaboom Books Bookstore. Contact them for more information through their website.

What about books in bad condition?

Recycle them! They are made of paper.

You are helping

Because of your donations, knowledge is being preserved and passed onto others. Because of you, someone is laughing at a funny passage or mastering another language. Thank you for making that possible. On the environmental side, while essentially all types of paper are biodegradable since paper comes from trees, they can take a long time to biodegrade and potentially release methane, an awful gas that significantly exacerbates climate change. Learn more about do's and don't's with paper recycling from the G Bioenergy blog.
