Compost, Compost, Compost!

You will do so much good

It does cost money - but it's worth it.

I cannot stress this enough: composting is one of the most beneficial things you can do for the environment and for yourself. It eliminates your food waste. It eliminates the guilt you may hold about throwing food away. Once the food is fully composted, it may come back full circle to fuel your garden, bolstering your flowers or vegetable plants.

If you live in Houston, you have several options:

    Zero Waste Houston: This composting service picks up your food waste weekly. For a residential subscription (🏡🪴😁), after pre-paying for a bucket ($25), it costs $10/month if you live inside the 610 loop, and $35/month if you live in the Woodlands or Bellaire outside of the 610 loop (read more about residential composting service options here). If you are a business looking to compost (📈☘️😄), find out more details here.

    Happy Earth Compost: This composting service picks up residential food waste weekly ($38/mo), bi-weekly ($27/mo) or monthly ($16/mo) [learn more about what each subscription includes here"]. It also offers commercial services with universities/schools, grocery stores, restaurants, corporate campuses/offices, and more. Learn more about partnering your business with Happy Earth here. Happy Earth also offers a recycling service in partnership with the company TerraCycle! In order to recycle with Happy Earth, you can purchase small, large, or extra large bags (ranging from $15-$35/mo), which will be dropped off at your house. Very important note: each bag that you buy can only contain one type of "waste stream," meaning item. For example, you can't put oral care packaging and Styrofoam in the same bag and send it to Happy Earth, no matter how big the bag is. You have to have oral care packaging and Styrofoam in separate bags. You can fill these bags with any of items that Happy Earth accepts for recycling. If you purchase a composting subscription with Happy Earth, you can leave out your TerraCycle bags on the composting pick-up day, and they will pick up the recycling too. If you only want to recycle with the bags, they can be dropped off at any of the following food markets: Memorial Village Farmers Market (Saturday/10840 Beinhorn Road/9am-1pm); Heights Mercentile (2nd & 4th Saturday of every month/714 Yale/9am-1pm); The Rice Village Farmers Market (1st & 3rd Sundays/2504 Amherst/9am-1pm); The Woodlands Farmers Market (Saturdays/Grogan's Mill, 7 Switchbud/9am-1pm)

    Moonshot Houston: This composting service does not pick up residential composting. However, it picks up food waste from commercial entities, like restaurants, businesses and schools. You do not have to clean the bin (like ZeroWaste)-they swap out clean "drums" each visit. They provide training for composting and track how much food businesses save on an easily accessible dashboard. Learn more about how to get involved with Moonshot here!

    For all of the above composting companies, the compost guidelines are simple: you can recycle all food (including meat, fish, and dairy, except for excessive fats/oils). For Happy Earth Compost, you can also compost paper towels and napkins.

    You can do compost food yourself, in your backyard. There are several methods to do this: to get an overview, go to this blog post from Sustainable(ish).

But what about the smell?

From my experience, the smell really shouldn't be a problem. As long as you seal the bucket and don't put excessive liquid substances in the compostable liners, you should be totally fine. Additionally, you can double or triple line the bucket to prevent smell. Obviously, the compost will smell because it's food waste, but especially with the liner it shouldn't be too bad. As a note, the compost liner will become damp to the touch after a few days inside the bucket, but I promise that it is not that unpleasant.

You are helping

Again: composting empowers you to help the planet! The benefits range from bolstering your garden to assisting in wetland reclamation to combatting climate change (learn more from the US Composting Council. The statistics about global food waste are troubling. The United Nations Environmental Programme estimates that 1.3 billion tonnes of food is wasted annually around the world. Did you know that more than 50% of landfills' greenhouse gas emissions in the United States come from food waste? Composting decreases methane and carbon emissions from landfills. It increases soil health and longevity, increasing the earth's ability to produce higher quality food. By composting, you guarantee that the valuable nutrients in your food will have a second life, enriching other plants and the world.
